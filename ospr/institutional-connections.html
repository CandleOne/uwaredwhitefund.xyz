Firm and university connections around algorithmic trading programs

How trading firms, technology vendors, and donors build lasting ties with universities through student trading programs. Descriptions come from the firms' and schools' own pages.

The biggest names

Leading trading firms and a major venture firm and how they are tied to universities. Most of these are sponsorships, gifts, and recruiting partnerships, not formal degree programs.

  • Traders@MIT: sponsor rosterOne student club's competition lists Citadel, Jane Street, Jump Trading, SIG, Optiver, Hudson River Trading, DRW, D.E. Shaw, Virtu, IMC, and Two Sigma as partners.
  • Jane Street funds Baruch College's math research programJane Street offered to sponsor the Baruch REU with $40,000 per year, alongside National Science Foundation funding.
  • Jane Street: $10 million gift to the Student Freedom InitiativeFunds HBCU student financing and extends Jane Street's FOCUS program to as many as 150 HBCU students per year.
  • Jane Street: university programs and eventsLists programs for current students, including FOCUS, WiSE, math and probability workshops, and a visiting researcher program for students and faculty.
  • Hudson River Trading sponsors Waterloo's math and computing contestsHRT gave to the University of Waterloo's CEMC to sponsor the Canadian Computing Competition and Olympiad, saying it wants to grow the community it hires from.
  • Hudson River Trading: UT Austin Computer Science industry partner profileHRT appears as an industry partner of UT Austin's CS department, with student opportunities listed.
  • Susquehanna (SIG) and Cornell's graduate careers officeA SIG researcher talk hosted as a partnership between SIG, Cornell Engineering Recruiting, and Careers Beyond Academia.
  • Susquehanna, IMC, and Radix as Princeton COS Con contributorsTrading firms listed among contributors to a Princeton computer science student conference.
  • DRW as a sponsor of the Rotman International Trading CompetitionDRW was a bronze sponsor of the University of Toronto's trading competition. From 2017.
  • a16z College Talent NetworkPer a third-party directory, a16z runs campus events at schools including Stanford, MIT, Georgia Tech, and Harvard. a16z is a venture firm, not a trading firm.
  • a16z backs the Horowitz Andreessen Academya16z put in $35 million of $42 million raised for a tuition-free program run alongside, not within, universities. Included as a contrast.

Firms donating technology to universities

The firm gives schools free platforms. In return it gets student familiarity with its tools and a recruiting channel.

  • Trading Technologies: TT CampusConnectDonates its trading platform at no cost to partner schools; nearly 100 universities in 13 countries take part, and the firm runs a summer internship program.
  • Carnegie Mellon joins the Trading Technologies programSoftware donated for CMU's computational finance program, with the firm's staff offering support to faculty.
  • Tulane Algorithmic Trading Club and Trading TechnologiesThe club runs contests in a university trading lab on donated software. The post is from 2013.

Sponsored competitions and events

Sponsors fund prizes, travel, and events, and in return get access to students.

  • University of Chicago Midwest Trading CompetitionThe university invited Trading Technologies to be lead sponsor; sponsor representatives speak to students about careers. From 2017.
  • Traders@MIT: aboutPartners with trading firms to host an intercollegiate competition of about 120 students, with sponsor mentorship and networking.
  • Traders at Berkeley: sponsorshipSponsor money funds prizes and travel; firms get coffee chats, career events, and resume book access.
  • QuantConnect: Quant LeagueA student algorithmic trading competition that connects university teams with funds seeking interns.
  • UC San Diego team wins a QuantConnect competitionA student quant club tied to the computer science department won the 2025 Q1 Open Quant League.
  • Imperial College London: AlgoSocRuns the Algothon hackathon and sponsor-firm speaker events, and is linked to the student-managed fund Queen's Tower Capital.

Programs that feed firms

Graduates and student fund members placed at trading and finance firms.

  • Baruch College: Master of Financial Engineering (Bloomberg profile)Describes Virtu Financial and Chicago Trading Company hiring from the program, with one firm saying Baruch does much of its filtering.
  • University of Alabama: Culverhouse Investment Management GroupThe group reports members and alumni receiving offers from firms including Jane Street and Point72.
  • Queen Mary University of London: QUMMIF success storiesLists fund alumni who moved into roles at UBS and State Street.

University and donor backing

The institution or its donors commit money to the program itself.

  • Samford University: trustees expand the Bulldog Investment FundThe Board of Trustees raised the fund to $1 million after strong early results. From 2009.
  • University of North Georgia: alumni fund the Student Investment ClubFour alumni gave $50,000 through the university foundation.

Clubs building partnerships with each other and with sponsors

Peer collaboration and published sponsorship plans.

  • Emory: Algory CapitalCollaborated with Georgia Tech's trading club on a competition and plans joint speaker and networking events.
  • ÉTS Montréal: AlgoÉTSA student algo trading club that publishes a partnership plan for sponsors. Canadian example.
